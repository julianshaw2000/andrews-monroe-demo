What the firm does

Employment matters handled here

Employment disputes are rarely only about law. They are about income, reputation and how you leave. The work below is done with that in mind — and with the deadline calendar open.

If you are an employee

  • Unfair dismissal — whether the reason was fair and whether a fair process was followed.
  • Redundancy — selection, consultation, alternative roles and statutory pay.
  • Discrimination — age, disability, race, religion, sex, sexual orientation, pregnancy and maternity.
  • Settlement agreements — reviewing terms, advising on value, negotiating where the offer is low.
  • Disciplinary and grievance hearings — preparing your case and your written statements.
  • Unpaid wages, holiday pay and deductions — including a series of shortfalls over time.
  • Tribunal claims — ACAS conciliation, ET1, witness statements and the hearing itself.

If you are an employer

  • Contracts, policies and handbooks — drafted or reviewed so they hold up when tested.
  • Disciplinary and grievance processes — running them so the outcome survives scrutiny.
  • Exits and settlement agreements — clean terms, properly documented.
  • Redundancy programmes — selection criteria, consultation and timetables.
  • TUPE — obligations on a transfer, information and consultation duties.
  • Defending tribunal claims — response, disclosure and hearing.

The time limits that matter

Most employment tribunal claims must be started within three months less one day of the event. Before issuing, you must notify ACAS for early conciliation, which pauses the clock while conciliation runs. Miss the limit and the tribunal can refuse to hear the claim at all — extensions exist, but they are discretionary and not something to count on.

Equal pay and statutory redundancy pay claims run to six months. Continuing discrimination may run from the last act rather than the first. This is exactly the sort of thing worth a phone call rather than a guess.

Common questions

Employment law, answered plainly

How long do I have to claim unfair dismissal?

Three months less one day from your effective date of termination. You must notify ACAS for early conciliation before issuing, and that period can pause the clock for up to about a month. Use the checker above for a starting figure, then confirm it against your own paperwork.

How long must I have worked there to claim unfair dismissal?

Ordinary unfair dismissal normally needs two years' continuous service. Some dismissals are automatically unfair with no qualifying period at all — for example where the reason relates to pregnancy, whistleblowing, trade union activity or asserting a statutory right. Discrimination claims have no minimum service requirement.

Do I need a solicitor to sign a settlement agreement?

Yes. A settlement agreement only binds you if you have taken independent legal advice on its terms and effect from a relevant adviser, who signs a certificate confirming it. Employers usually contribute towards that cost, and the contribution is normally agreed before the work is done.

What could I recover if a claim succeeds?

For unfair dismissal, compensation usually has two parts: a basic award based on age, length of service and weekly pay, and a compensatory award for lost earnings and benefits. The compensatory award is capped at the lower of 52 weeks' pay or a statutory maximum. Discrimination and whistleblowing awards are not capped and can include compensation for injury to feelings.

Will I have to go to a hearing?

Most cases settle before a full hearing, often during ACAS conciliation or shortly after a claim is issued. Where a hearing is needed, the firm is authorised to exercise rights of audience and conduct litigation, so representation does not have to be handed to someone new.

Do you act for employers?

Yes. The firm advises employers on contracts, staff handbooks and policies, disciplinary and grievance processes, redundancy programmes, settlement agreements, TUPE transfers and defending tribunal claims.

Acting for both sides is not a conflict — it is what makes the advice useful, because it anticipates the argument coming back the other way. The firm will not, of course, act for both parties in the same dispute.

What will it cost?

A standard settlement agreement is £600. Negotiated agreements typically run £1,000 to £3,000, and a medium-complexity tribunal claim £3,500 to £15,000. The standard hourly rate is £303. All figures exclude VAT and disbursements, and your own written estimate comes before any work starts. Full price transparency.
