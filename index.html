Common questions

Straight answers, before you call

How long do I have to bring an employment tribunal claim?

Most claims must be started within three months less one day of the event. For unfair dismissal that runs from your effective date of termination.

You must notify ACAS for early conciliation first, which can pause the clock for up to about a month. Some claims run to different limits, so check the date against your own facts — the case review tool above gives you a starting figure.

Do I have to contact ACAS before making a claim?

Yes, for most employment tribunal claims. ACAS early conciliation is compulsory, and ACAS issues a certificate at the end of it. The conciliation period can extend your time limit, but skipping the step will normally stop your claim from being accepted at all.

Do you act for employers as well as employees?

Yes — both. For employees: dismissal, redundancy, discrimination, settlement agreements and tribunal claims. For employers: contracts, policies and handbooks, disciplinary and grievance processes, exits, settlement agreements and TUPE.

What does a settlement agreement cost?

A simple employment dispute that settles early is charged at a fixed fee of around £700. Where negotiation is needed, expect £1,000 to £3,000 depending on complexity. The firm is not VAT registered, so no VAT is added to its fees, though disbursements may carry VAT charged by the supplier. Where your employer is contributing to your legal fees, that is factored in from the start.

Where is the firm based, and do you act outside London?

The office is at 330 High Holborn, London WC2A 1HL, in the legal district beside Chancery Lane. The firm acts for clients across England and Wales, and most matters are handled by phone, video and email. Appointments in person are by arrangement.

Is what I tell you confidential?

Yes. Anything you tell a solicitor about your matter is confidential, including at the enquiry stage and whether or not you go on to instruct the firm. That said, please keep your first message brief and avoid sending documents until the firm has confirmed there is no conflict and that it can act for you.

How do I complain if something goes wrong?

Raise it with the firm first and it will be handled under the firm's written complaints procedure, which is provided at the start of your matter. If you are still unhappy you can take the complaint to the Legal Ombudsman, normally within six months of the firm's final response. Concerns about a solicitor's professional conduct can be reported to the Solicitors Regulation Authority.
