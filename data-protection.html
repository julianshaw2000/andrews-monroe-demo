Data protection policy

How the firm handles personal data

The standards the firm holds itself to when processing personal data, and the rights you have over information it holds about you.

Last reviewed 11 August 2026 · next review due August 2028

This is the firm's policy, not its privacy notice. This page says how the firm handles personal data generally. If you want to know what is held about you specifically, why, and for how long, that is the privacy notice.

Scope

This policy applies to all personal data processed by Andrews & Monroe Solicitors, whether it relates to clients, to people on the other side of a matter, to witnesses, to suppliers or to staff. It forms part of the firm's approach to compliance with data protection law, and anyone handling personal data on the firm's behalf is required to follow it.

The law that applies

In this policy, data protection law means the UK General Data Protection Regulation and the Data Protection Act 2018, together with the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations where they apply, as amended from time to time.

The firm acts as a data controller for the personal data it holds about clients and enquirers, and in some circumstances as a processor for data held on a client's behalf.

The principles

Personal data will be:

  1. processed lawfully, fairly and transparently
  2. collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes, and not used in a way incompatible with those purposes
  3. adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary
  4. accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date, with inaccurate data erased or corrected without delay
  5. kept in a form allowing identification for no longer than is necessary
  6. processed securely, including protection against unauthorised processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage

The firm is accountable for these principles and for being able to demonstrate that it meets them.

In practice

  • The lawful basis for processing is identified before the processing starts.
  • Nothing is done with your data that you would not expect from this policy and the privacy notice.
  • Only the personal data actually needed is collected.
  • Data is held only as long as it is needed, then securely destroyed, in line with the firm's retention policy.
  • Access is limited to those who need it, and data is held and transferred securely.

Obtaining, accessing or disclosing personal data in breach of this policy may be a criminal offence as well as a disciplinary matter.

Special category data

Some personal data needs extra protection: data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, genetic or biometric data used to identify someone, health, and sex life or sexual orientation. Data about criminal convictions and offences is subject to its own further protections.

Employment, discrimination and family work routinely involves this kind of data. The firm processes it where that is necessary for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims, or in the course of providing legal advice, and applies the safeguards set out in this policy to it.

Your rights

You have the right to:

  • Be informed about how your data is used — that is what the privacy notice is for.
  • Access a copy of the personal data held about you, together with the purposes of the processing, the categories of data, who it has been or will be disclosed to, the retention period, the source if it did not come from you, whether any automated decision-making is involved, and your right to complain to the Information Commissioner.
  • Rectification of inaccurate data.
  • Erasure, where the data is no longer needed, consent is withdrawn and no other basis applies, there is no lawful basis for the processing, or there is a legal obligation to delete it.
  • Restriction of processing while accuracy is contested, where processing is unlawful but you do not want the data erased, where you need the data for a legal claim, or pending verification of an objection.
  • Portability of data you provided, where processing is based on consent or on a contract and carried out by automated means.
  • Object to processing based on legitimate interests, unless the firm can show compelling grounds that override your interests, or the processing is for legal claims.

Requests are handled without undue delay and within one month of receipt. If a request is complex, or there are several, that period can be extended — you will be told if so, and why.

These rights are not absolute. Where the firm must keep records — under anti-money-laundering rules, at its regulator's requirement, or because the information is needed to bring or defend a legal claim — a request may properly be refused in whole or in part. You will be told which exemption is relied on.

Keeping data, and sending it abroad

Files are kept after a matter closes for the period set by the firm's retention policy, which reflects limitation periods, insurance requirements and regulatory obligations, and are then securely destroyed.

The firm does not routinely transfer personal data outside the United Kingdom. Where a matter requires it — an international children case, or a party or expert based abroad — any transfer is made only with an appropriate safeguard in place.

Security and personal data breaches

The firm maintains technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk, and keeps a record of any personal data breach and of the reasoning applied to it. Where a breach is likely to result in a risk to people's rights and freedoms it is reported to the Information Commissioner's Office without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of the firm becoming aware of it. Where the risk is high, the people affected are told as well.

Who is responsible

The principal of the firm takes ultimate responsibility for data protection. The firm is a recognised sole practice and is not required to appoint a Data Protection Officer; Johanna A. Cargill is the data protection lead and the point of contact for anything on this page.

Complaints

If you are unhappy with how your personal data has been handled, raise it with the firm first, using the details above — it can usually be sorted out quickly. If you remain dissatisfied you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk, which is the UK's independent supervisory authority for data protection.

Complaints about the firm's service rather than its data handling go through the complaints procedure instead.

Related policies

This policy sits alongside the firm's record of processing activities, its privacy notices, its personal data breach reporting process and breach register, its retention policy, its data subject rights procedure, its data protection impact assessment process and its IT security policies. Those are internal documents; copies of the parts that affect you can be requested.

Review

This policy is monitored and reviewed at least every two years, and whenever the law or the firm's practice changes materially.

Ask about your data directly

Subject access requests, corrections and deletions are handled by the solicitor herself. There is no form and no ticket number.
