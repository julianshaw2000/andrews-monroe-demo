Privacy & cookies

What this site collects

Almost nothing, and none of it automatically. This page explains what happens to your information on the website, and separately how the firm handles personal data as your solicitor.

Last updated 8 August 2026

The short version. This website sets no cookies, runs no analytics, stores nothing in your browser and has no database. Its forms do not send anything anywhere — they open your own email app with a message ready for you to review and send. Nothing on the page is loaded from another company's servers, so no third party is told that you were here.

What the website itself does

Cookies

This site sets no cookies of any kind — not necessary ones, not analytics, not advertising. There is no cookie banner because there is nothing to consent to. If you ever see a cookie banner on this domain, something has changed and this page should have changed with it.

Browser storage

Nothing is written to localStorage, sessionStorage or any other browser storage. The deadline checker and the case review form work entirely in the page's memory and are gone the moment you close the tab. If you refresh the page, whatever you typed is lost — that is deliberate.

Analytics and tracking

There are none. No analytics product, no tag manager, no advertising pixels, no social media trackers, no session recording, no fingerprinting.

Forms

The case review form and the call-back form do not transmit anything to this website or to any third-party form service. When you press send, the details you entered are assembled into a draft email and handed to your own email application. Nothing is sent until you press send in that application, and you can edit or discard it first. Once you do send it, the email reaches the firm's inbox and is handled as described below.

Fonts and other third parties

There are none. The site's typefaces are served from this website itself rather than from Google Fonts or any similar service, so loading a page makes no request to any other company and discloses your IP address to nobody but this site's host. There are no embedded maps, videos, chat widgets, social buttons or advertising scripts, because each of those would be a third party watching you read.

If a typeface fails to load, the page falls back to your system's own fonts and remains completely readable.

Hosting and server logs

The site is served by a third-party hosting provider which, in common with all web hosts, may record technical request data such as IP addresses in its server logs for security and operational purposes. The firm does not use those logs for analytics or marketing.

Personal data the firm holds

Separately from the website, the firm holds personal data in the course of giving legal advice.

What is held

  • Identification and contact details — name, address, date of birth, telephone, email, and identity documents required by anti-money-laundering rules.
  • Matter information — the facts of your case and the documents and correspondence relating to it.
  • Financial information — billing details and, where relevant, information about your means or assets.
  • Special category data — where your matter requires it. Employment, discrimination and family work frequently involves data about health, ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation or similar.

Why it is held, and on what lawful basis

  • To perform a contract — carrying out the work you have instructed the firm to do.
  • To comply with legal obligations — including client identification and record-keeping requirements imposed on solicitors.
  • Legitimate interests — running and administering the practice, keeping records for insurance and regulatory purposes, and responding to enquiries.
  • For special category data — principally that the processing is necessary for the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims, or is carried out in the course of providing legal advice.

Who it is shared with

Your information is confidential and is not sold, rented or used for marketing. It is disclosed only where necessary for your matter or where the firm is required to disclose it — for example to barristers instructed on your case, to the other side and the court or tribunal in proceedings, to experts, to the firm's insurers and auditors, and to regulators and law enforcement where the law requires it. Solicitors are subject to reporting obligations under anti-money-laundering legislation which may, in defined circumstances, prevent the firm from telling you that a report has been made.

How long it is kept

Files are retained after a matter closes for a period set by the firm's retention policy, reflecting limitation periods, insurance requirements and the firm's regulatory obligations, after which they are securely destroyed. The specific period applying to your matter is confirmed in your client care letter.

Transfers outside the UK

The firm does not routinely transfer personal data outside the United Kingdom. Where a matter requires it — for example an international children case or a party based abroad — any transfer is made with appropriate safeguards in place.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you have the right to:

  • ask for a copy of the personal data held about you
  • have inaccurate data corrected
  • ask for data to be erased, where there is no continuing reason to hold it
  • ask that processing be restricted while a concern is resolved
  • object to processing carried out on the basis of legitimate interests
  • receive certain data in a portable format
  • withdraw consent, where processing is based on consent

These rights are not absolute. Where the firm is required to keep records — by anti-money-laundering rules, by its regulator, or because the information is needed for the establishment or defence of legal claims — a request to erase may properly be refused. You will be told which exemption applies and why.

To make a request, write to Johanna Cargill using the details above. There is normally no charge, and a response is given within one month.

If you are not satisfied. You can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, the UK supervisory authority for data protection, at ico.org.uk. The firm would rather you raised it directly first, so it can be put right. Complaints about the firm's service rather than its data handling go through the complaints procedure.
