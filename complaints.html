Complaints

If something has gone wrong

Tell the firm first. Most complaints are the result of something that can be put right quickly, and you have a right to escalate if it is not.

Last updated 8 August 2026

In short. Complain to the firm first. If you are not satisfied with the firm's final response, or eight weeks pass without one, you can take the complaint to the Legal Ombudsman. Concerns about a solicitor's professional conduct go to the Solicitors Regulation Authority instead.

Step one — tell the firm

Raise the problem with Johanna Cargill, by telephone on 07521 844993, by email to jc@andrewsmonroe.co.uk, or in writing to the office address. As a sole practice there is nobody to be passed to — the person who handled your matter is the person who will deal with the complaint.

It helps to set out what happened, when, what you would like done about it, and to attach anything relevant. If you would rather put it in writing than discuss it, that is fine.

A complaint in writing is preferred, because email can go astray — but email is accepted, and if it is easier to telephone in the first instance, do that.

Complaining does not affect how your matter is handled, and there is no charge for it.

Step two — the Legal Ombudsman

If you are not satisfied with the firm's final response, or if eight weeks pass from the date you first complained without the firm resolving it, you can ask the Legal Ombudsman to look at the complaint. The service is independent, and free to the person complaining.

The time limits

You normally need to refer the complaint to the Legal Ombudsman:

  • within six months of receiving the firm's final response to your complaint; and
  • no later than one year from the act or omission complained about, or from when you should reasonably have known there was cause for complaint.

The Ombudsman can, in limited circumstances, exercise discretion to accept a complaint outside those limits. Full details and the scheme rules are published at legalombudsman.org.uk.

What the Ombudsman deals with

The Legal Ombudsman deals with complaints about service — delay, poor communication, failure to follow instructions, costs information, and the way a matter was handled. It can direct an apology, a reduction in fees, the return of documents, or compensation.

Step three — the Solicitors Regulation Authority

The SRA regulates solicitors and deals with conduct rather than service — dishonesty, discrimination, breach of the SRA Principles or the Codes of Conduct, and misuse of client money. That is a different question from whether you received a good service, and it goes to the SRA directly rather than through the Ombudsman.

Reports can be made through sra.org.uk/consumers/problems/report-solicitor. The firm's SRA number is 489238.

Complaints about a bill

If your complaint is about an invoice, you may also have a right to apply to the court for an assessment of the bill under the Solicitors Act 1974. Time limits apply to that route, and the Legal Ombudsman may not consider a complaint about a bill where the court is already assessing it. If you are unhappy with a bill, say so promptly rather than waiting.

Data protection complaints

Complaints about how the firm has handled personal data are dealt with under the privacy policy and can be taken to the Information Commissioner's Office rather than the Legal Ombudsman.

Raise it directly, and early

A complaint is easier to resolve while the matter is live than after it has closed. It will not affect how your case is handled.
